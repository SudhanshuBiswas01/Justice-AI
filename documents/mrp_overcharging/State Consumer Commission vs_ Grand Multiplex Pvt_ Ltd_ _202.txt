TITLE: State Consumer Commission vs. Grand Multiplex Pvt. Ltd. (2022)
SOURCE: all
CATEGORY: mrp_overcharging
TYPE: Judgment
============================================================

Before the State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, Delhi.
Case No. Appeal/512/2022.
Decided on: September 14, 2022.

Mr. Amit Sharma (Complainant) vs. Grand Multiplex Pvt. Ltd. (Respondent).

JUDGMENT:
The complainant, Mr. Amit Sharma, visited the respondent's multiplex cinema hall on June 12, 2022, and purchased a bottle of packaged mineral water. The Maximum Retail Price (MRP) printed on the bottle was Rs. 20, but the respondent charged the complainant Rs. 60. The complainant raised an objection, but the respondent refused to sell the bottle at the printed MRP, citing "service charges" and "internal pricing policy for premium facilities".

The complainant filed a case before the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Forum, which ruled in favor of the complainant. The respondent appealed this decision to the State Commission.

LEGAL PRINCIPLES:
Under the Legal Metrology Act, 2009, and the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011, it is unlawful to sell any packaged commodity at a price exceeding the Maximum Retail Price (MRP) declared on the package. Section 36 of the Act prescribes penalties for selling commodities above the declared price.

Furthermore, the Supreme Court of India in the case of 'Federation of Hotel and Restaurant Associations of India vs. Union of India' clarified that hotels and restaurants are service providers and may charge above MRP when serving food/drink within a sit-down restaurant as part of a service. However, a cinema counter selling packaged goods over the counter does not fall under this exception. This constitutes "unfair trade practice" under Section 2(47) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.

DECISION:
The State Commission finds that charging Rs. 60 for a Rs. 20 mineral water bottle at a cinema counter is a clear violation of the Legal Metrology Act and constitutes an unfair trade practice. The appeal is dismissed. The respondent is directed to:
1. Refund Rs. 40 to the complainant with interest at 9% per annum.
2. Pay Rs. 15,000 as compensation for mental harassment and litigation costs.
3. Deposit Rs. 50,000 in the Consumer Welfare Fund for overcharging consumers at scale.
